# The founding-customer offer

**Send in October, to every tester who has used Weeksolved for real.**

The point of this is not revenue. It is to find out, before you spend a dollar on filing
fees, whether a pharmacy manager will *commit* to paying. Compliments are free. A yes in
writing is not. Three of those and Phase 3 goes ahead on schedule; nought or one and we
talk about price, positioning or product before any money is spent.

Ask for a reply either way. A no with a reason is worth more than silence.

---

## The offer itself

**$290 a year — the Solo annual price — locked for as long as they keep subscribing, on
whatever plan they end up needing.** If they grow into Pro at $490, they still pay $290.
If they add locations, they still pay $290. Nothing is charged until launch day, and they
can walk away at any point before it without owing anything.

Why it works: it costs you nothing today, it is unambiguously a better deal than anyone
will get later, and it asks for a decision rather than an opinion.

## The email

> **Subject: Something I'd like to ask you before Weeksolved goes live**
>
> Hi [name],
>
> You've been using Weeksolved for a few weeks now, and the schedule you've been building
> with it is the reason it works as well as it does — nearly everything that's changed
> since you started came from something you or one of the other testers hit.
>
> It goes live in January. I want to ask you something before it does.
>
> When it opens, Weeksolved will be $29 a month for a single location, and $49 for the
> version with everything in it. I'd like to offer you **$290 for the year — and that
> price stays yours for as long as you keep using it.** Not an introductory rate that
> quietly rises: the same $290 in year three, on whatever plan you end up on, even if you
> add locations.
>
> There's no catch and nothing to pay now. You wouldn't be charged until the day it opens,
> and if you change your mind between now and then, you tell me and that's the end of it.
>
> What I'm actually asking for is a straight answer. If Weeksolved is worth paying for,
> I'd like to know now, while I can still do something about it. If it isn't — or not yet —
> I'd genuinely rather hear that, and hear why. That's more useful to me than a polite yes.
>
> Just reply "in" or "not yet". A sentence on why, either way, would help me a lot.
>
> Thanks for the last few weeks. It's a better product than it would have been.
>
> [your name]
> weeksolved@gmail.com

## Notes on sending it

- **One at a time, addressed to them.** Not a BCC blast. These are people who have done
  you a favour; a group email reads like a campaign and will get a campaign's response rate.
- **Only to testers who have actually used it** — someone who took a code and never signed
  in cannot give you a meaningful answer, and asking them dilutes the signal.
- **Don't discount further if someone hesitates.** The number you are trying to learn is
  whether the product is worth $290, not what it takes to close a friend. If they say no,
  the useful next question is "what would it need to do?"
- **Write the answers down** the day they arrive, in their words. In January you will want
  the exact phrasing, both for pricing and for the landing page.

## What the answers mean

| Yeses in writing | What it tells you | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 3 or more | People will pay. The product is not the risk any more. | Phase 3 on schedule — file the LLC in the December window. |
| 2 | Promising, thin. Probably a reach problem, not a product one. | Proceed, but line up more testers before launch. |
| 0 or 1 | Something is wrong, and it is cheaper to find out now. | Stop. We look at price, at who you're selling to, and at what the noes said. No filing fees yet. |

Nought or one is not a failure. It is the plan working — that is exactly the outcome this
phase exists to catch, at a cost of one email instead of a year and a few thousand dollars.
