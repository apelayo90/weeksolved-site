Why flat beats per-employee

Most scheduling apps charge $2.50–$5 per employee per month and require every staff member to install an app and keep an account. Weeksolved is built the other way around: the manager runs it, staff just receive their schedules — by text, email, print, or straight into their phone calendars.

The math for an 18-person team: per-seat apps run $45–$90 every month, and grow every time you hire. Weeksolved Solo is a flat $29 — break law included, $24 if you pay yearly — and the same on the day you hire your 30th person.

Fair questions

Is it really free right now?

Yes. During the tester program everything is free — every feature, no card, no time limit beyond your access code's window. When billing opens, testers get first claim on a founding price locked for as long as they stay subscribed.

What happens when the trial ends later on?

When billing opens: 30 days free, no card up front, every feature on. If you don't continue, your schedule data stays exportable — nothing is held hostage.

Do my employees need accounts?

No — that's the point. Only the manager signs in. Staff get their schedules however they already live: text, email, print-out, or a calendar file that drops shifts into their phone.

Is the break-law engine really on the cheapest plan?

Yes, and on the free trial. A single missed meal break in a state like California costs an hour of premium pay — per person, per day — so putting the thing that prevents it behind a higher price would be selling you the problem and charging for the cure. Every plan places every meal and rest break inside the legal window while it drafts, and prints the week so you can check it.

Then what is Pro for?

Floors that are more complicated than a simple schedule. Named posts that cover each other's meal breaks, work patterns like seven on seven off, one fair draw for the season's vacations, PTO balances, license expiry, and the record of who got what. If your week is a straightforward set of shifts, Solo is the whole product.
