What's new

The latest changes, in plain words. Weeksolved ships continuously, so this page keeps the recent few and lets the rest go. Items marked Asked for by a tester came from somebody using it and telling us.

Recent changes

Backups carry your board, not its keys. A backup file no longer includes the codes your reply forms are checked against or the settings that say where mail and reports go; restoring a file already dropped them, so nothing changes for a restore. Accounts that sign in with Google can now delete themselves, from Settings and from the end-of-trial screen. On a phone the delete dialog’s messages come into view and a long email address no longer pushes it sideways; the end-of-trial screen puts Back up now first when a change has not reached your account. The Week tools that rebuild a week are off while it is posted; checking a trade no longer moves anyone’s meal; the fix sheet never offers a meal time earlier than the rules on this board allow.

A split shift’s meal is no longer planned inside its unpaid break. When a split shift’s unpaid break began exactly at the meal’s latest start, the drafter could put the meal inside the break. Nothing said so, and an unpaid meal came off the shift’s hours on top of the break. Now the latest start on such a shift is the minute work resumes, and the meal goes in working time. The shift editor plans a split shift’s meals around its break too, so Save no longer moves one into it. A week drafted before this update keeps its meals until its shifts are planned again — Draft with Keep only the shifts I’ve locked, a change to the meal settings, or Place the breaks again on the fix sheet — and a posted week stays as posted; on both, the week’s checks now name a meal that sits inside a split shift’s break. Also new: when a test window ends, the screen that says so offers Back up now and Delete my account, so you can take your board, or close the account, from that screen.

The two nights a year the clocks change. A shift that runs across the change is an hour longer or shorter than its times suggest. Weeksolved already put that hour into the shift’s hours and overtime. When you draft a week it now plans meals and rest breaks on the time the shift is scheduled at work too, and a line on the week says so. A split shift that crosses the change, a start or end time in the hour that happens twice or is skipped, and a week drafted before this update each get a line of their own. A posted week is left as posted.

Moving through a page with the keyboard keeps the control in view. On a phone or in a small window, a control you move to with the keyboard is no longer hidden behind the bottom bar: the page scrolls far enough to show it. The same goes for the list of fixes in the fix sheet, which no longer slides under the button at the bottom of the sheet.

Plainer wording, two new report columns, bigger tap targets on a phone. Settings, Help and the setup questions now say what Weeksolved does with the rules on your board. The meal-length setting reads What the rule gives, and on a board with no meal rule it says so. The report builder has two more columns to tick, Paid meal min and Time worked, so hours paid and time worked sit side by side. A new account needs a password of 10 characters or more; signing in to an account you already have is unchanged. On a phone, the setup questions, Settings, Help, Export and Analytics have bigger tap targets, and the smallest labels on a desktop are a size larger. Also fixed: in the shift editor, ticking waived on a meal changes the words beside it at once, and the calendar button on a date field is named properly for screen readers.

Meals can be paid. Asked for by a tester. Settings → Meals and rest breaks has a new first question, Are meal periods paid here? Answer Yes — the meal is paid and a meal’s minutes stay in paid hours: a 7:00 to 3:00 shift reads 8 hours, not 7½, and totals, guaranteed hours, contracts and cost include them. A paid meal counts toward hours paid and cost. The overtime and shift-length checks keep counting time worked, as they do now. Meals are still placed, staggered and checked the same way. Drafted weeks follow at once, without a meal moving; a posted week stays as posted until you unpost it. Setup asks the same question on Your shifts, and the shift list export gains a last column, Paid meal min. A shift sized for an unpaid meal pays more once meals are paid, so someone on an exact contract may draft to fewer shifts until the shift is shortened. Also fixed: search now finds every Help entry — the last few had been left out — and the names box on the setup team step sits higher on a small phone.

Team, Requests and the day sheet now read like the rest of the app. Team is one roster in alphabetical order with the paperwork that needs you first, a find bar once you pass five people, and the people set aside, your staff groups and the whole-team tools as lists under it. A person opens in a sheet over the page, with Save changes pinned at the bottom; close it with something typed and unsaved and it asks first. Requests has two parts, Requests and Time off. The first group is Waiting on you — the requests the draft couldn’t honour and the shifts handed back — and it is the same number Home shows; the rest are grouped by when they fall, each with its outcome on the row, and a request opens in a sheet where Decline is a row rather than a button in the corner. Time off moved here from Team, and a leave’s status and Remove sit on its own row; Draft the schedule lives on Week and replies are read on Home, so each thing has one place. The day sheet opens on the date with arrows either side of it, a back link that names the screen you came from, and the same status line as Home. Where coverage dips, what needs fixing and what is worth a look are rows you can press; with something to fix, Fix is the one filled button, otherwise Hand it out is. The lanes are quieter, with the coverage lane last, and the day’s other actions — the posts, redoing the sheet, a PDF — are a short list below. On a board with more than one location, Team, Requests and the day sheet all follow the location you have chosen, and Clear under Gone by only removes requests that have actually ended, and only the ones on screen.

A posted week stays posted, and the drafter keeps to more of your rules. A week that has gone up can no longer be rebuilt from the shape menu, the search box, the fill-mode switch or the Priority page — each says the week stands as posted and that a change by hand is what alters it now. When the drafter swaps two people it now judges them at the location they would actually work, so two people you keep apart are no longer put together there; it no longer sends a per diem home when that would leave someone on their own; two shifts on one day are compared in the right order for rest between shifts; a shift on a day you are closed is refused and named rather than drafted; the night the clocks change, rest is measured in real hours; and when a ratio rule would refuse every open slot only because of where the meals fell at drafting time, the finished week is asked again and the slot fills. A Keep until or Lengthen fix that would cost one of your rules now says so on its own line — and is never the recommended one.

The setup questions say what they mean, and the week screen is quieter. From the first outside tester: the time-off question now asks plainly whether you approve longer vacations one by one or run one draw for the season; someone still learning the job is either scheduled as an extra — on top of the people you asked for, never alone — or counted like anyone else, in those words everywhere; a meal waiver is explained before you are asked how to use it, and its three settings have one name each; and the team step now says, where you are looking, that each name gets a row below for full or part time and hours a week. On the week, the fix dialog is now a sheet like the others, the countdown after you press Post says things once and keeps its two buttons where they belong, the clutter below the seven days is gone and What this week needs opens the one list of things to fix, and the links that belonged on the website no longer sit at the foot of the app. Dialogs close with the same round ✕ as the sheets, the Theme and Motion switches and every dialog button are full-size targets, and with high-contrast colours on, the week’s bars and marks stay visible.

The tab bar always says where you are. On a phone, a page opened from Tools now lights the Tools tab, a page opened from Week or Home lights the tab you came from, and Settings and Help light your avatar, so one press always takes you back. Dialogs close with the same round ✕ as the sheets, and the buttons along the top and bottom of every dialog, along with the Theme and Motion switches, are now full-size targets. Week has one Shape control instead of two. On a desktop the sidebar is the one list of pages, its smallest text is easier to read, and it lights Settings and Help while they are open. With high-contrast colours turned on, the current tab and the warning marks stay visible, and at the largest text sizes the tab labels no longer run into each other. Also fixed: a shift put on by hand at a location with no opening hours of its own is flagged on the days that location is shut, and the line suggesting room at another location no longer skips someone who has a real opening there.

A holiday answer comes first. When someone’s holiday answer says they can’t work a holiday, the draft no longer puts them on it — not for the day’s cover, not to make up their guaranteed hours, and not on a night shift that runs into the holiday — whether or not that holiday had a draw. When you want them there all the same, the check offers one press to keep them on it anyway, and everyone you have held to a holiday is listed on the Holidays page with a Remove beside each. With more than one location, the answers already on file show on the Holidays page too, with a Clear beside each, and when a holiday rolls over to next year the holds made for it go with it.

Each location looks after its own people. On a board with more than one location, topping someone up to their guaranteed hours now happens only at the locations they work, and on a day only another location is open, that location’s own people are topped up there. When someone is still short and only a different location has room, a line says so and offers one press to add them there, showing first anything it would cause. On a day the main location is shut, the whole-board minimums count only the locations that are open; a dip in cover is placed at the location whose hours cover most of it, and Need one more adds to that location’s row. A shift put on by hand at a location on a day its hours keep it shut is flagged, and adding a shift by hand starts on a location that is open that day, with closed ones marked. Also fixed: the day view’s On the floor bar now lines up with the hour marks above it; it was stretched across the chart and could sit up to an hour off.

A holiday no longer fills up twice. When a holiday’s draw went but its drawn shifts stayed on a drafted week, a new draft could add the day’s usual cover on top of them. The checks now say so — “More on than the day asks for” — with one press to take the extra drawn shifts off, and the Holidays page says what the drafted week still holds. When someone is on a holiday they say they can’t work, the fix takes off every shift of theirs that day in one press, and a drawn shift you hand to someone else is no longer described as coming from the draw. The line about a busy stretch now reads true on every board: it compares the rush with the busiest part of the rest of the day.

More of the app reads each location’s own hours. On a board with more than one location, the busy-stretch check, the day view’s lanes, the rows proposed from your posts and the holiday checks now use each location’s own opening hours, so a location open on Saturday is looked after on Saturday and an evening location’s rush is judged in its evening. A holiday whose cover is set location by location no longer draws a line saying the weekday has no coverage needs, and a holiday’s own Coverage needed says when the shift it uses runs outside the holiday’s hours.

Holiday draws hold up when plans change. Two people drawn for the same holiday who can’t be left alone are now placed together, instead of both being turned away with a reason that wasn’t true. When someone drawn for a holiday leaves the roster, or their answer later says they can’t work it, the message now names the draws that go, and the day drafts the way the holiday is run. Anyone still on a holiday shift they now say they can’t work is named in the checks, with one press to take the shift off. On a board with more than one location, changing one holiday no longer clears the draws of the others. A trainee is no longer added to a holiday set to add nobody extra.

Checks read the right hours. A shift asked for only on holidays is judged against the hours those holidays keep, so a 9–3 holiday shift on a 9–3 holiday is no longer called outside your hours, and one that runs past a holiday’s early close is. On a board with more than one location, the check for times entered the wrong way round, the pre-draft coverage check and the longest-day check each use every location’s own hours, and a trainee listed at two locations is placed only where it is clear which one, with a line asking for a home location otherwise. Remove everything now clears the person from the app’s lists as well as their shifts, and says plainly which written records keep their name.

Each location keeps its own hours and its own holidays. Asked for by you. On a board with more than one location, each location’s own opening hours now decide its own days. A location open on Saturday is drafted and checked on Saturday even when the main one is shut, and a location that closes at 5 is no longer told nobody is on its floor until 9. A holiday can be answered location by location — closed, normal hours, or holiday hours of its own — so the office can close while the after-hours site opens, and each location is drafted and checked against its own answer. The holiday poll is off on these boards; weeks already drafted keep the people drawn. A board with one location drafts and checks exactly as before.

Business & rules and Scheduling rules now read as a summary. Both pages state what is in force in plain words, with one Change in Settings link per card that opens the matching card. Meal times and the lunch closure moved into Settings → Meals and rest breaks, so every setting is changed in one place, and the backup and start-over buttons live under Settings → Account. Settings now shows a value saved outside its usual list as it is, instead of showing the first option. Also fixed: the holiday top-up setting no longer promises to fill an empty floor, which the drafter stopped doing some time ago; a draw warning clears once the person is on the schedule; and a shift asked for at a second location is judged against that location’s hours, not the main one’s.

Shifts with a longer meal now fill. When a shift carried its own unpaid meal of 45 or 60 minutes, the drafter still priced it at the board’s 30 when it checked each person’s hours — so a 7:00–4:00 shift that pays 8 read as 8½, a fifth one looked like it broke a 40-hour week, and full-timers on exactly 40 stopped at 32 with shifts left open. Every hour check now reads the shift’s own meal, the unpaid middle of a split shift, the night the clocks change and the person’s age, the same way the finished week does. The same fix reaches a “keep me under” hours request and the under-18 day and week limits, which had been calling an 8-hour shift with a long meal over an 8-hour cap. Also fixed: a night shift typed as 7 PM to 7:30 AM is no longer asked whether it meant PM; Sunday can copy Saturday under Coverage needs; a report’s total adds the exact minutes rather than the rounded rows, and says so; the top of the day sheet no longer says coverage holds all day while a must-cover post sits empty; and on a board with more than one location, a callout, its cover, a template and a shift you add by hand all stay at the location they belong to.

A first draft that comes out clean. Asked for by you. Setup now asks how long a shift is in paid hours — 6, 8, 10 or 12 — and builds every shift from that, adding the unpaid meal your state’s break rules take out, so an 8-hour shift in California runs 8½ hours on the clock and pays 8. Each shape prints its times and what it pays, and the one that fits your day is marked “Suggested”. Round the clock, the shifts now overlap by the meal and each pays the full 8 (or 12). Where a length cannot land on a full-timer’s 40 hours, or a shorter weekend shift gets in the way, the screen says so before you draft, and 10- and 12-hour shifts in a state with a daily overtime line say how much of each is overtime. The sizing screen compares the hours your team is contracted for with the hours your cover asks for, says what the first draft will add, and offers Raise the cover to match with an Undo. The drafter no longer puts a full-timer on an exact contract onto a short weekend shift someone else could take when that shift would leave their hours out of reach; when nobody else can take it, the check says so in one sentence. And Back up now — the same words on every backup button — hands a phone’s backup to the share sheet (Save to Files, AirDrop, Mail) instead of opening it as a page of text.

The smaller things from the same list. A post left unheld is now marked at its hour on the day sheet, the same mark a short minimum gets, and pressing it opens that post’s fix. A rule that needs a licensed person on site names the per diem who could cover the hour. The overnight guess — “Did you mean 5 PM?” with a one-press fix and It’s right as entered — now sits on opening hours, holiday hours and the setup questions, not only the shift editor. Each shift’s own meal length reaches every hour and cost preview. When someone calls out, the slot says it was released from a lock, whoever covers it starts unlocked, the callout line travels with the cover, and the cover sheet offers the same swap the week’s fixes offer — never the person who called out. Every button that does something is drawn as a button; only Close and Cancel stay quiet. Check copy names the meal window and the latest start the rule allows, and the settings section is now Business & rules. Taking a screenshot or turning the phone no longer throws a scrolled dialog back to the top. A short hourly minimum now reads in one plain sentence — “Tuesday 2–3 PM has 2 on the floor; the minimum is 3”. And the city scheduling-ordinance checks have moved behind an Early features switch under Settings, off unless you turn it on; a board already using them keeps them. When someone calls out on a posted week, the cover sheet now asks first — Ask Amara writes a question, not a schedule change, and the slot remembers who you asked and when; Said yes — put them on places them and sends a thank-you instead. Both messages are yours to reword under Settings → Messages.

Only your coverage needs bring people in. A post left unheld or an hour that dips under your minimum is now something worth a look — the hour is marked on the day sheet and one press adds somebody for that hour if you want them — rather than a reason for the drafter to pull in one more person on its own. That pass is gone. What is red is what breaks a rule you set for this board, or a coverage row left short. Project time is handed out only when there is nobody left to cover with, it never goes to the same need a per diem was brought in for, and it is labelled “Project time” wherever it appears. A pharmacist whose clerk work is switched off can no longer be drafted onto a clerk-only row for any reason. A row can now have a floor of zero, for the shifts that exist only as an outlet for spare people.

Per diem is decided person by person. The board-wide per-diem switch is gone. Each on-call person carries their own setting — Call them in automatically when nobody else can cover, or Ask me first — and everyone starts on Ask me first. When a row cannot be filled from your core staff, the open item names the per diem who could take it, and the fix is one press. The sentence that used to say hour, rest or consecutive-day limits blocked every candidate is replaced by the true reasons, person by person.

Someone called out. Asked for by you. Home now carries Someone called out whenever today or tomorrow has a shift that has not started. Pick the person, press once, and the shift is released and the same cover sheet the app uses for requests opens — who could take it and what each would break, the per diem who could cover it named first, offer it, swap, or leave it open. On a posted week nothing is placed without your press, and any change you send carries a line reminding you that some city rules add notice or pay when a posted week changes. The count of callouts on a person’s card is gone: each entry lives only on the shift it happened to, and nothing that drafts or ranks reads it.

A slip in the setup is named before its symptoms. One start time typed as 9:15 PM instead of 9:15 AM used to produce a dozen red items and none of them pointed at the cause. The shift editor now says “Runs overnight — 20 h 30 min. Did you mean 9:15 AM?” with a one-press fix (or one that says it is right as entered, for a shift that really does run overnight), the Shifts list says “overnight” beside the times, and on Home the one thing to fix leads with the likely slip and says how many other items correcting it clears — a number the app works out by trying the correction, not by guessing. Shifts also carry length presses (6, 8, 10 or 12 paid hours) and their own unpaid meal (the board default, 45 or 60 minutes), printed wherever the shift appears when it is anything but the default.

Setup on a phone, fixed where it pinched. Leaving the setup questions asks on its own row instead of inside the button bar. The team step gives every person two rows with Type, Min and Max labelled and readable. Remove is a button, the guessed shift preference is a caption, and picking an option no longer throws the screen back to the top — nor does taking a screenshot. The holiday question offers the holiday itself and all three days. New people start from their type: full-time exactly 40, part-time 20–40, per diem, temp and seasonal 0–40, training 20–40. Closing for lunch can be set for only the days you pick, for 30, 45 or 60 minutes inside the window, in setup as well as under Rules. The Week screen can discard its own draft, the day sheet and Add someone sit at the top of the day as buttons, and a cross-month week no longer wraps its date range.

The week screen, the tiles and the rest of Home now speak one language. On a phone the Week screen opens with the date range and its arrows, the state of the week, the four figures, and one line of verdict before the seven days — seven plain rows you can read at arm’s length, each with its hours, how many are on, and a mark if that day carries something to fix. The week’s tools sit under one menu instead of a second row of buttons. On a desktop the grid is one surface with hairlines rather than boxes, and each shift now shows its meal time in the cell, because that is what a meal-period warning is about. Home’s four tiles line up at one size, and the rest of the page — the next six weeks, handing the week out, replies from your team, saved reports — is grouped under plain headings as rows a thumb can hit. Posting a week with something open now uses the same sheet as everything else in the app. Nothing on either screen is set smaller than eleven pixels any more.

Things move the way you expect. Buttons press. Screens arrive rather than snap. Sheets slide up on a phone and in from the side on a desktop. When you fix the one thing on Home, the card gives way to the next one without the buttons under it moving, and when a week goes up the seven bars fill in left to right. Every movement is short — nothing longer than a quarter of a second — and if your phone or computer is set to reduce motion, or you switch it off under your avatar → Appearance → Motion, the states still change and nothing moves. A fix that changed the week now offers Undo right in the message that names the change.

Home is the week. Home now opens on the week itself — seven bars, one per day, whose height is that day’s opening hours and whose fill is how much of the cover you asked for is met, with a mark at the hour anything falls short. A drafted week is hatched; a posted one is solid, so the state reads from across the counter. Above it, one line says where the week stands and how many things need you; below it, the one thing to fix in the same words the checks use, then the press that posts. Everything that used to stack above the schedule — notices, tips, counts — is one tap away under that line, and nothing marked to fix or worth a look is ever reduced to a count. Posting a week with something still open goes through one screen that shows what is open and what was checked, above the button; a clean week posts in one press.

One way to each place. On a phone the tabs are Home, Week, Requests, Team and Tools; Tools holds the pages that are not this week’s schedule, in the same groups and the same order as the sidebar on a desktop. The menu button, the second More and the extra search boxes are gone. Your account, Settings, Help and Report a problem live behind the avatar on a phone and at the foot of the sidebar on a desktop — one door each, and every page is listed for everyone, so there is no longer a switch that hides some of them. Everything reachable before is still reachable. Also fixed: the setup title no longer wraps one word per line on a phone, the progress bar counts the steps its label counts, the week’s date range sits on one line, and descriptions under card titles wrap properly on narrow screens.

Name the job right where you add the people. Asked for by you. On the setup screen where you type your team’s names, each column now has the job name in an editable box directly above it, pre-filled with our suggestion, so you can rename it on the spot instead of scrolling back. Two more asks landed with it: a holiday can now count as busier itself (Settings → Holidays: The holiday itself or The holiday and the days either side), and a business that gives longer lunches can set the unpaid meal period to 45 or 60 minutes under Meals and rest breaks — paid hours and the second-meal timing follow, and where the rule on your board gives a shift a longer meal, the longer one is used.

A guarantee the shifts cannot reach is explained once, with the arithmetic. When a person’s guaranteed hours cannot be met by any whole number of the shifts you run — a 36-hour guarantee, a 40-hour cap and 12½-hour shifts that pay 11½ once the unpaid meal periods come out, so three shifts land at 34½ and four at 46 — the checks panel used to say “under guaranteed hours” for every such person, every week, with no way to clear it. It now says it once per contract, names how many people share that contract, shows the numbers, says what the draft did about it, and points at the setting the paid hours depend on. It does not tell you what to do about your contract. Weeksolved is scheduling software; it is not a law firm and does not give legal advice.

An empty floor at one location is no longer hidden by a staffed one. With several locations, the check for “nobody on the floor” and the pass that fills such a hole were asking about the whole business at once, so one busy location answered for an empty one. Each location is now asked about on its own — and only on days that location has something scheduled, so a place with nothing on the sheet that day is not treated as a hole to fill. One location behaves exactly as before.

The report builder no longer shows a cost or an overtime column. Those two columns priced a week differently from the board’s own budget figure — per shift, with no weekly or seventh-day line — so the same week could show one number on the board and a lower one in a report. Rather than show a figure that can disagree with itself, the report builder now shows hours only; the budget chip on the board keeps the correct cost. Hours reports also stay inside the dates you ask for: a 1st-to-15th run reports the 1st to the 15th, not every week that touches it.

California is the starting point, and shifts have one length. A new board starts on California rules rather than federal ones, and meal waivers start switched on where the California rules on this board allow them, so the first draft is judged by the rules you actually work under. Open-ended shifts — a start or end that moved inside a range — are gone: every shift runs the length you set, and any saved shift that was open-ended now runs its usual length. They caused more corrections than they saved. Wording that said “compliant” or “compliance” now says what the app does — it checks hours against your rules — not what it certifies.

Plainer wording about what leaves your device, and a switch for the one thing that does. Three sentences in the app were out of date and read better than the truth. Weeksolved needs an account — there is no signed-out mode, and the privacy card said there was. The page contacts two Google servers, not one: www.gstatic.com serves the sign-in code and Firebase holds your board, and the live What leaves this device panel now names both by reading the page you are actually running. And when the app hits an error it files a crash report without asking — the error, the screen you were on, your browser and screen size, your state, and how many people, roles and shifts are on the board, never a name and never any part of a schedule. That is now said where it belongs, and Settings → Automatic crash reports switches it off, in which case nothing is sent at all. Nothing about what the app does changed; what changed is that it now describes itself accurately.

Several places to schedule, asked for in setup. Asked for by you. Where do you operate? now asks how many places you schedule and lets you name them, before the hours question — because hours are the first thing that differs between sites. Each place gets its own opening hours and its own coverage; a person’s profile says where they can work, and leaving it blank means anywhere, which is how a floater or a provider shared across sites is handled. One place is one press and the screen looks as it always did. Setup can add locations and never removes one, so answering “just the one” over a board with five leaves all five alone.

“Is this the same business?” Asked for by you. Running the setup questions again over a board that already has people on it now asks first whether this is the same business or a different one. Keeping it is the preselected answer and behaves exactly as before. Choosing a different business empties the form as well as the board, so the old roster is not sitting in the boxes waiting to be typed over, and the last screen counts out what is going — people, roles, shifts, coverage, posts, groups, locations and every drafted week — before anything happens. Your account, your sign-in and how the app looks come through untouched.

More than four roles, and departments explained. Asked for by you. The roles screen in setup takes up to twelve and has an Add another role button, so a kitchen with a GM, two manager grades, crew and trainees fits without finishing setup first. If you run departments, they are roles: nobody is ever scheduled into a role they do not hold, so departments keep to themselves without anything to manage.

Fewer presses to put someone back, or take them off. Asked for by you. A person who has been set aside now has Remove on their row rather than only inside their profile, and a whole list can be cleared in one dialog once it is long enough to be a chore. The Send beside “problem reports ready to go out” in the More panel now opens the report — it was doing nothing.

Choose the days extra hours may land on. Suggested by you. Settings → Hours, guarantees and patterns has a row of day boxes that decide where hours owed on a guarantee may go: untick the weekend and hours owed are made up Monday to Friday, so a Saturday you want kept lean stays lean. If the days you ticked cannot take the hours, the drafter uses the next open day and the week’s checks say so, naming the day. Every day stays ticked unless you change it, so existing boards draft exactly as they did.

Google sign-in from the Home Screen app. Asked for by you. Weeksolved added to an iPhone’s Home Screen offers Continue with Google alongside email and password. Google’s answer comes back through weeksolved.com itself, so the installed app completes the exchange inside its own storage. Google opens in a sheet and brings you back; if a particular iPhone cannot, the screen says so and email and password still work.

A person’s name opens their profile. Asked for by you. On the Week grid and the day sheet a name is a link to that person’s profile — availability, hours, roles — so checking why somebody keeps landing on a particular day is one press instead of a trip to the Team page. Every shift name on the day sheet opens that shift the same way.

PDFs that hold everything you asked for. Asked for by you. A multi-week PDF is one page per week, day sheets are one page per day for every day you picked, and the file is named for what is in it. The individual printed schedules and every week or day page carry the holiday’s name and its hours in the heading, and every page gets its footer. On a phone or a Home Screen install a PDF opens in a new tab instead of on top of the app, so closing it brings you back where you were; a desktop downloads it. Page counts, sheet counts, the header bytes and the new-tab path are all under test.

A holiday is its own kind of day. Asked for by you. Week and day views show the holiday’s own hours (or “closed”), and a holiday sits out the busy/quiet checks. A holiday can say which posts must be staffed that day — the same posts as usual, or only the ones you tick. Holidays you enter yourself repeat every year unless you say otherwise; once one has passed, the page offers to roll it forward, and the standard ones move to their proper date on their own. The draw is the answer to who works that holiday: the people it picks go on locked at the holiday’s own hours, nobody else is added, and a redraft leaves it alone. The fair split uses chance to break a tie between people with the same load, everybody who can take a holiday gets a first before anyone gets a second, a “must work it” note wins, and “Worked out again” genuinely re-draws. If somebody who was drawn cannot work it — approved leave, an expired licence, an hour cap — you are told who and why, and the place is left open for you to fill. Ranking is by drag or arrows, in the app and in the poll file.

Required cover comes first. Asked for by you. Required coverage is filled before anybody is topped up to guaranteed hours, and days with the fewest people able to cover them are filled first — so the one person who can cover a Saturday “exactly 1” is kept for it, and the top-up pass looks across the whole week for an open required slot before it deepens anything. An “at least” row is a floor, not a licence to swallow the day: while a day still has an opening somebody could work, they take it before any already-satisfied shift gets deeper. When nobody left can work the open shift, hours owed are made up elsewhere rather than spent on a slot nobody could fill. A shift the day does not ask for is not used to deepen that day — a shift the day asks for outranks one it does not. And when a part-timer comes up short of hours, the reason you are shown is the one that actually applies.

Save & next. Asked for by you. Every editing dialog that comes in a list — holidays, shifts, people, posts, coverage rows — has Save & next, so working down a list is one press per item instead of save, close, find, open.

Sometimes the answer is to take a shift off. Asked for by you. When a week has too much on it, the first thing offered is taking a shift off — the one that lands closest to the contract, rehearsed before it happens so you see what follows — then shortening one, then handing one to somebody with room. Being over an hour cap, over the overtime line or past a days-in-a-row limit offers removal too. An exact contract holds when shifts are placed as well, so a holiday week stays inside it.

A paid holiday counts toward guaranteed hours. Asked for by you. Hours paid for a holiday count toward a guarantee or a fixed contract, and the week shows hours worked and hours paid side by side. A holiday somebody is rostered to WORK counts once: those hours are already in the week, and the holiday premium is money on top. An hour cap is unchanged either way, because a cap is about hours on the floor. If your guarantee is a promise of hours actually worked, there is a switch for that under Settings → Hours, guarantees and patterns.

A day only runs the shifts you asked it to run. Asked for by you. A shift missing from a day’s Coverage needs means the business does not run it that day, so a Saturday whose Coverage needs say “Weekend RPH, exactly 1” runs exactly that. Two exceptions, because they are you saying it out loud: a shift marked prefer this one for extra hours, and the named shift on the Rules page, may still be used anywhere. And when somebody cannot reach their guaranteed hours inside the shifts their days actually run, you are told they are short and why — no shift is invented to close the gap.

Posts stay where you put them. Asked for by you. Two post settings hold wherever they are set: All day — one post per person keeps linked posts from swapping at midday, and one person holds this all day means one holder, all day. Rotate with is now Swap places with, part-way through the day, which is what it does; the setting that gives people variety across the week is Numbered posts → Share one rotation.

The app fetches almost nothing from anyone. The typefaces and the library that writes PDFs are carried inside the page. That matters most for PDF export, which now holds up on a network that blocks a public CDN — a corporate proxy, a content blocker, hotel wi-fi. Text arrives in its own font on the first paint, and nobody outside is told that you opened the app. One request is left, to the account service itself, and Settings → What leaves this device reads that off the running page rather than taking our word for it.

Holidays: say what actually runs. Asked for by you. Holiday cover is the eighth day on Coverage needs: under Sunday to Saturday there is a Holidays block, edited by the same dialog as every other day, with the same “exactly this many” meaning, and one press turns it on. The cover figure you enter on the holiday itself drives the draft and caps it, the way a holiday’s own how you run it beats the general setting. Every holiday added from the standard list arrives with a setting of its own — Christmas closed, Veterans Day normal — and a holiday’s own setting beats the general one; the general setting tells you how far it reaches (“used by 3 of 11”) and offers to clear the individual ones so your choice takes. Holiday hours are time pickers rather than typed text, one button sets the same hours on every holiday at once, and Minimum cover only means a holiday gets exactly what you asked for and nobody else, not even for hours owed. The week’s checks name what you got either way: a holiday drafted like an ordinary weekday says so.

Leave reasons stay private. They stay off every shared schedule and export, and a decision about someone’s leave is sent to that person alone.

Nothing goes out in half, and deletes clean up after themselves. A week too long to send by email or text holds rather than sending half, and says what to do instead. Deleting a role clears every post, shift, ratio rule, pattern and holiday that pointed at it, and deleting a shift releases the posts pinned to it. And moving your week boundary keeps day-sheet notes, approved overtime, staff claims and the week’s pattern.

A new shift is eight hours plus the meal. Asked for by you. Type 8:00 and the shift ends at 16:30 — eight paid hours plus the unpaid meal, set to your state’s rule. Move the start and the end follows it; type your own end and it stops following, so a deliberate twelve-hour shift stays twelve hours.

Fewer places to look. Asked for by you. Six Set up menu rows are now one Schedule setup with tabs — shifts and roles, coverage needs, who gets first pick, posts on the floor. Time away joins Requests as a tab, so time off has one front door instead of five. Business & rules and Scheduling rules are a statement of what is in force, and Settings owns the editing, so the two say the same thing. Every old link, fix button and search result still lands exactly where it did.

Busy stretches, and a menu that makes sense. Asked for by you. You can say when a day is busy, not just that it is: Coverage needs → a day → Set a busy stretch. Like the busy-day label it decides where the people you already have go, rather than adding anybody, so hours owed land on a shift that covers the rush, and the checks tell you when the rush is the thinnest part of the day. Alongside it, a full pass over the menus: Settings is seven cards named for what they hold, each group named for one thing instead of two, and on the phone Settings and the day sheet sit at the top of the menu.

Search actually searches. Asked for by you. Settings holds 89 rows, and search reads across every one of them, whichever group you are on — “pay period” finds the pay period rows wherever they live. A row can also be found by the name of the card it sits in, so “business name” finds it. Results appear from the first character, narrow as you type, and go straight there when you press one.

Exports, checked at real size. Asked for by you. Tested against 150 people and twelve drafted weeks — 6,672 shifts. A long table repeats its column headings on every page and breaks between rows, on every printed schedule and day sheet. And a custom report leaves as a proper Excel workbook — real number cells so hours add up, a frozen bold heading, your subtotals and total, and a second sheet recording what the report covered and when it was run. CSV is still there if you want it.

Coverage needs opens on what the week you are looking at is drafted from. Asked for by you. If a week is on a pattern, the page opens on that pattern’s set, so a role you add, remove or change on a shift is written where Generate reads it. Edit a different set deliberately and the page says so at the top, with one press to switch back. Alongside it: when every row on a shift is exact, that is the whole shift — nobody is added beside them to make up somebody else’s hours.
