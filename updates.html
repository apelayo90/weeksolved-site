What's new

The latest changes, in plain words. Weeksolved ships continuously, so this page keeps the recent few and lets the rest go. Items marked Asked for by a tester came from somebody using it and telling us.

This week

Choose the days extra hours may land on. Suggested by you. Settings → Hours, guarantees and patterns has a row of day boxes that decide where hours owed on a guarantee may go: untick the weekend and hours owed are made up Monday to Friday, so a Saturday you want kept lean stays lean. If the days you ticked cannot take the hours, the drafter uses the next open day and the week’s checks say so, naming the day. Every day stays ticked unless you change it, so existing boards draft exactly as they did.

Google sign-in from the Home Screen app. Asked for by you. Weeksolved added to an iPhone’s Home Screen offers Continue with Google alongside email and password. Google’s answer comes back through weeksolved.com itself, so the installed app completes the exchange inside its own storage. Google opens in a sheet and brings you back; if a particular iPhone cannot, the screen says so and email and password still work.

A person’s name opens their profile. Asked for by you. On the Week grid and the day sheet a name is a link to that person’s profile — availability, hours, roles — so checking why somebody keeps landing on a particular day is one press instead of a trip to the Team page. Every shift name on the day sheet opens that shift the same way.

PDFs that hold everything you asked for. Asked for by you. A multi-week PDF is one page per week, day sheets are one page per day for every day you picked, and the file is named for what is in it. The individual printed schedules and every week or day page carry the holiday’s name and its hours in the heading, and every page gets its footer. On a phone or a Home Screen install a PDF opens in a new tab instead of on top of the app, so closing it brings you back where you were; a desktop downloads it. Page counts, sheet counts, the header bytes and the new-tab path are all under test.

A holiday is its own kind of day. Asked for by you. Week and day views show the holiday’s own hours (or “closed”), and a holiday sits out the busy/quiet checks. A holiday can say which posts must be staffed that day — the same posts as usual, or only the ones you tick. Holidays you enter yourself repeat every year unless you say otherwise; once one has passed, the page offers to roll it forward, and the standard ones move to their proper date on their own. The draw is the answer to who works that holiday: the people it picks go on locked at the holiday’s own hours, nobody else is added, and a redraft leaves it alone. The fair split uses chance to break a tie between people with the same load, everybody who can take a holiday gets a first before anyone gets a second, a “must work it” note wins, and “Worked out again” genuinely re-draws. If somebody who was drawn cannot work it — approved leave, an expired licence, an hour cap — you are told who and why, and the place is left open for you to fill. Ranking is by drag or arrows, in the app and in the poll file.

Required cover comes first. Asked for by you. Required coverage is filled before anybody is topped up to guaranteed hours, and days with the fewest people able to cover them are filled first — so the one person who can cover a Saturday “exactly 1” is kept for it, and the top-up pass looks across the whole week for an open required slot before it deepens anything. An “at least” row is a floor, not a licence to swallow the day: while a day still has an opening somebody could work, they take it before any already-satisfied shift gets deeper. When nobody left can work the open shift, hours owed are made up elsewhere rather than spent on a slot nobody could fill. A shift the day does not ask for is not used to deepen that day — a shift the day asks for outranks one it does not. And when a part-timer comes up short of hours, the reason you are shown is the one that actually applies.

Save & next. Asked for by you. Every editing dialog that comes in a list — holidays, shifts, people, posts, coverage rows — has Save & next, so working down a list is one press per item instead of save, close, find, open.

Sometimes the answer is to take a shift off. Asked for by you. When a week has too much on it, the first thing offered is taking a shift off — the one that lands closest to the contract, rehearsed before it happens so you see what follows — then shortening one, then handing one to somebody with room. Being over an hour cap, over the overtime line or past a days-in-a-row limit offers removal too. An exact contract holds when shifts are placed as well, so a holiday week stays inside it.

A paid holiday counts toward guaranteed hours. Asked for by you. Hours paid for a holiday count toward a guarantee or a fixed contract, and the week shows hours worked and hours paid side by side. A holiday somebody is rostered to WORK counts once: those hours are already in the week, and the holiday premium is money on top. An hour cap is unchanged either way, because a cap is about hours on the floor. If your guarantee is a promise of hours actually worked, there is a switch for that under Settings → Hours, guarantees and patterns.

A day only runs the shifts you asked it to run. Asked for by you. A shift missing from a day’s Coverage needs means the business does not run it that day, so a Saturday whose Coverage needs say Weekend RPH, exactly 1 runs exactly that. Two exceptions, because they are you saying it out loud: a shift marked prefer this one for extra hours, and the named shift on the Rules page, may still be used anywhere. And when somebody cannot reach their guaranteed hours inside the shifts their days actually run, you are told they are short and why — no shift is invented to close the gap.

Posts stay where you put them. Asked for by you. Two post settings hold wherever they are set: All day — one post per person keeps linked posts from swapping at midday, and one person holds this all day means one holder, all day. “Rotate with” is now Swap places with, part-way through the day, which is what it does; the setting that gives people variety across the week is Numbered posts → Share one rotation.

The app fetches almost nothing from anyone. The typefaces and the library that writes PDFs are carried inside the page. That matters most for PDF export, which now holds up on a network that blocks a public CDN — a corporate proxy, a content blocker, hotel wi-fi. Text arrives in its own font on the first paint, and nobody outside is told that you opened the app. One request is left, to the account service itself, and Settings → What leaves this device reads that off the running page rather than taking our word for it.

Holidays: say what actually runs. Asked for by you. Holiday cover is the eighth day on Coverage needs: under Sunday to Saturday there is a Holidays block, edited by the same dialog as every other day, with the same “exactly this many” meaning, and one press turns it on. The cover figure you enter on the holiday itself drives the draft and caps it, the way a holiday’s own how you run it beats the general setting. Every holiday added from the standard list arrives with a setting of its own — Christmas closed, Veterans Day normal — and a holiday’s own setting beats the general one; the general setting tells you how far it reaches (“used by 3 of 11”) and offers to clear the individual ones so your choice takes. Holiday hours are time pickers rather than typed text, one button sets the same hours on every holiday at once, and Minimum cover only means a holiday gets exactly what you asked for and nobody else — not for hours owed, and not to fill the floor. The week’s checks name what you got either way: a holiday drafted like an ordinary weekday says so, and one carrying more people than you asked for explains that the floor still has to be covered and points at holiday hours.

Leave reasons stay private. They stay off every shared schedule and export, and a decision about someone’s leave is sent to that person alone.

Nothing goes out in half, and deletes clean up after themselves. A week too long to send by email or text holds rather than sending half, and says what to do instead. Deleting a role clears every post, shift, ratio rule, pattern and holiday that pointed at it, and deleting a shift releases the posts pinned to it. And moving your week boundary keeps day-sheet notes, approved overtime, staff claims and the week’s pattern.

A new shift is eight hours plus the meal. Asked for by you. Type 8:00 and the shift ends at 16:30 — eight paid hours plus the unpaid meal, set to your state’s rule. Move the start and the end follows it; type your own end and it stops following, so a deliberate twelve-hour shift stays twelve hours.

Fewer places to look. Asked for by you. Six “Set up” menu rows are now one Schedule setup with tabs — shifts and roles, coverage needs, who gets first pick, posts on the floor. Time away joins Requests as a tab, so time off has one front door instead of five. Business & law and Scheduling rules are a statement of what is in force, and Settings owns the editing, so the two say the same thing. Every old link, fix button and search result still lands exactly where it did.

Busy stretches, and a menu that makes sense. Asked for by you. You can say when a day is busy, not just that it is: Coverage needs → a day → “Set a busy stretch”. Like the busy-day label it decides where the people you already have go, rather than adding anybody, so hours owed land on a shift that covers the rush, and the checks tell you when the rush is the thinnest part of the day. Alongside it, a full pass over the menus: Settings is seven cards named for what they hold, each group named for one thing instead of two, and on the phone Settings and the day sheet sit at the top of the menu.

Search actually searches. Asked for by you. Settings holds 89 rows, and search reads across every one of them, whichever group you are on — “pay period” finds the pay period rows wherever they live. A row can also be found by the name of the card it sits in, so “business name” finds it. Results appear from the first character, narrow as you type, and go straight there when you press one.

Exports, checked at real size. Asked for by you. Tested against 150 people and twelve drafted weeks — 6,672 shifts. A long table repeats its column headings on every page and breaks between rows, on every printed schedule and day sheet. And a custom report leaves as a proper Excel workbook — real number cells so hours add up, a frozen bold heading, your subtotals and total, and a second sheet recording what the report covered and when it was run. CSV is still there if you want it.

Coverage needs opens on what the week you are looking at is drafted from. Asked for by you. If a week is on a pattern, the page opens on that pattern’s set, so a role you add, remove or change on a shift is written where Generate reads it. Edit a different set deliberately and the page says so at the top, with one press to switch back. Alongside it: when every row on a shift is exact, that is the whole shift — nobody is added beside them to make up somebody else’s hours.

Earlier

September 7, 2026

  • A small ? on anything that needs one. Every setting and every field whose name does not explain itself carries a round ?. Press it and you get what it does, what changes if you move it, and — wherever a number makes it clearer — a worked example.
  • Guaranteed hours are met by the drafter. A profile that promises someone 16 hours comes back with 16 hours on it, built from ordinary shifts added where you choose — busiest days first, or evenly through the week.
  • “Monday is my busiest day, so why is it my thinnest?” The week says so itself: if your busiest day asks for fewer shifts than a quieter one, or two days you marked equally busy are staffed very differently, you get one line explaining that a busy label decides where people go rather than adding any.
  • Exports and printouts carry the whole picture. Somebody removed from the roster with their record kept appears on the paperwork for the weeks they worked, in line with the totals, and a shift that runs past midnight prints its finish time.
  • People you set aside stop asking for hours. Somebody set aside is off the roster, so their guaranteed hours are left alone while they hold no shifts. What is reported instead is a set-aside person who still holds a shift.
  • Less on the screen at once. The person and shift dialogs keep the everyday fields in view and fold the rest under “More”; the week’s review card and the Team page tuck their secondary buttons into a menu; Home shows two notices and folds the rest.
  • Time away, and reports you build yourself. A new Time away page lists who is off or asking for what, coming up and gone by, with the date and time each request was entered and what happened to it. Under Analytics you can build your own report from shifts, hours, requests or leave.
  • On the phone: a side menu that slides in, and a sheet that scrolls. The ☰ button at the top opens the full menu with search, and slides away when you pick something. The More sheet scrolls on iPhone, and Coverage needs can add a row to Saturday and Sunday together.
  • Two means two. A new Coverage needs row is exact unless you open it up — the dialog asks plainly whether the drafter may add more than you asked for. Rows that say “at least” are named at the top of the page with one button to make them all exact.
  • Pay periods you can see at a glance. Turn pay periods on under Settings and every other period is shaded — on the week grid, the phone view, the week cards, All schedules, and on the printed sheets, with a small key beside the title. A week that straddles two periods shows exactly where one ends and the next begins, and you can tell Weeksolved which month your fiscal year starts in.
