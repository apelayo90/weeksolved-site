What's new

The latest changes, in plain words. Weeksolved ships continuously — most tester reports become a fix the same day — so this page keeps the recent few and lets the rest go. Items marked Asked for by a tester came from somebody using it and telling us.

This week

PDFs that hold everything you asked for. Reported by you. Asking for four weeks gave you one page; asking for the whole week’s day sheets gave you one day. The choice was read correctly and then thrown away by an export that only ever knew how to draw a single page. A multi-week PDF is now one page per week, day sheets are one page per day for every day you picked, and the file is named for what is in it. On a phone or a Home Screen install a PDF now opens in a new tab instead of on top of the app, so closing it brings you back where you were; a desktop still downloads it. The individual printed schedules and every week or day page now carry the holiday’s name and its hours in the heading, and every page — not just the last — gets its footer. All of it is now under test: page counts, sheet counts, the header bytes and the new-tab path.

A holiday is its own kind of day. All reported by you. The week showed a holiday running normal hours when it had been set to shortened ones, and stamped it busy or quiet as though it were an ordinary Tuesday. Week and day views now show the holiday’s own hours (or “closed”), and a holiday skips the busy/quiet checks entirely. A holiday can now say which posts must be staffed that day — the same posts as usual, or only the ones you tick. Holidays you enter yourself repeat every year unless you say otherwise; once one has passed, the page offers to roll it forward, and the standard ones move to their proper date on their own. The fair split now uses chance to break a tie between people with the same load, and “Worked out again” genuinely re-draws; nobody gets a second holiday until everyone who can take one has had a first, and a “must work it” note always wins. Ranking is by drag or arrows, in the app and in the poll file.

Saturday’s required shift before Monday’s extras, and Save & next. Both reported by you. The one person who could cover a Saturday “exactly 1” kept being spent on a weekday before the drafter ever reached Saturday, because days were filled Monday first. Days with the fewest people able to cover them are now filled first, and the top-up pass looks across the whole week for an open required slot before it deepens anything. A part-timer’s shortfall was also being explained with a reason from the wrong pass (“they’re set to stay on the floor”) — the real reason is kept now. And every editing dialog that comes in a list — holidays, shifts, people, posts, coverage rows — has Save & next, so working down a list is one press per item instead of save, close, find, open.

Sometimes the answer is to take a shift off. Reported by you. Two halves of one report. A 40-hour contract came back at 48 hours — 40 on the floor plus 8 hours of holiday pay. The check had learned that holiday pay counts toward a contract; the passes that aim at one had not, so the drafter correctly stopped at 32 and then a later pass stretched the shifts back up to 40. Both halves now ask for the same number, and an exact contract holds to it when shifts are placed as well, so a holiday week cannot be filled past it. The second half: every suggestion offered was to ADD, for somebody who already had too much. Being over a contract and being under it were treated as the same problem. Now, when the week has too much on it, the first thing offered is taking a shift off — the one that lands closest to the contract, rehearsed before it happens so you see what follows — then shortening one, then handing one to somebody with room. Being over an hour cap, over the overtime line or past a days-in-a-row limit offers removal now too.

The holiday draw decides who works the holiday. Reported by you. Two faults, and the first was silent. The step that puts the people your draw picked onto the holiday was asking the wrong question — it called the routine that words a refusal rather than the one that decides it, and that routine always has something to say, so every person the lottery drew was turned away, every time, with an unhelpful reason. With the winners gone, the pass that tops people up to their guaranteed hours then filled the day with whoever happened to be short — people who had never entered the draw, working the wrong hours. Both are fixed: the draw is now the answer to who works that holiday, the winners go on locked at the holiday’s own hours, nobody else is added, and a redraft leaves it alone. If somebody who was drawn genuinely cannot work it — approved leave, an expired licence, an hour cap — you are told who and why, and the place is left open rather than quietly handed to a stranger.

A paid holiday counts toward guaranteed hours. Reported by you. Five full-timers in a week containing one holiday all reported 32h of 40h, every one of them set up with 8 hours of holiday pay. 32 plus 8 is 40 — not one of them was short. The week’s hours were counted from shifts alone, so a paid holiday was invisible both to the check and to the drafter, which then chased a gap that did not exist and would have taken them to 48. Hours paid for a holiday now count toward a guarantee or a fixed contract, and the week shows both figures side by side. A holiday somebody is rostered to WORK is never counted twice: those hours are already in the week, and the holiday premium is money on top. An hour cap is unchanged either way, because a cap is about hours on the floor. If your guarantee is a promise of hours actually worked, there is a switch for that under Settings → Hours, guarantees and patterns.

A day only runs the shifts you asked it to run. Reported by you. A Saturday whose Coverage needs said Weekend RPH, exactly 1 came back with six people: one on Weekend RPH and five on a weekday shift Saturday never asks for. The cap was doing its job — a ceiling is written per shift, and there was no row for that shift on Saturday at all, so it had no ceiling of any kind and the pass that tops people up to their guaranteed hours filled the day with it. A shift missing from a day’s Coverage needs now means the business does not run it that day, rather than that it runs without limit. Two exceptions, because they are you saying it out loud: a shift marked prefer this one for extra hours, and the named shift on the Rules page, may still be used anywhere. And when somebody cannot reach their guaranteed hours inside the shifts their days actually run, you are told they are short and why — no shift is invented to close the gap.

A holiday gets the cover you asked for, and nothing rotates that you told to stay put. All reported by you. A holiday set to fewer people asking for one pharmacist was drafting ten: the figure you enter on the holiday itself drove the holiday poll and nothing else, while the drafter read a different, empty setting — so the day asked for nobody, and the passes that fill an empty floor and top people up to their guaranteed hours filled it for you. The figure on the holiday now wins, the way a holiday’s own how you run it already beats the general setting, and a holiday that states its own cover is capped at it. Separately, two post settings were being ignored: All day — one post per person did not stop linked posts swapping at midday, and one person holds this all day did not either. Both hold now, wherever they are set. And “Rotate with” is renamed Swap places with, part-way through the day — it was being read as “these two count as the same kind of work”, which is a different setting (Numbered posts → Share one rotation) and the one that actually gives people variety across the week.

The app now fetches almost nothing from anyone. Opening Weeksolved used to pull three things off the internet: the typefaces from Google Fonts, the library that writes PDFs from a public CDN, and the sign-in service from Google. The first two are now carried inside the page. That matters most for PDFs: on a network that blocked that CDN — a corporate proxy, a content blocker, hotel wi-fi — PDF export simply stopped working, and there was nothing on screen to tell you why. It cannot now. You also stop seeing the moment where text loads in the wrong font, and nobody outside is told that you opened the app. One request is left, to the account service itself, and Settings → What leaves this device reads that off the running page rather than taking our word for it. The privacy and security pages have been corrected to match: they used to name three companies, and one is the truth.

Required cover comes first. Reported by you. A Monday asking for two on C, one on E, two on G and at least four on D came back with ten people on D and nobody at all on C, E or G. Coverage needs is filled first and then a second pass tops people up to their guaranteed hours — and that second pass was allowed to pile onto the “at least” row while the exact ones stood open. An “at least” row is a floor, not a licence to swallow the day: while a day still has an opening somebody could work, they take it before any already-satisfied shift gets deeper. When nobody left can work the open shift — the usual reason it is still open — hours owed are still made up elsewhere rather than thrown away on a slot nobody could ever fill. The same pass also drew from every shift you have with all of them equally likely, so a shift named for Saturday could turn up on a Monday beside the four shifts Monday actually runs; a shift the day asks for now outranks one it does not. And every shift name printed on the day sheet opens that shift, so “what is this?” is one press rather than a hunt.

Holidays, properly. Reported by you. Every holiday added from the standard list arrives with a setting of its own — Christmas closed, Veterans Day normal — and a holiday’s own setting beats the general one, so switching the general one could change nothing and say nothing about it. Weeksolved now tells you the truth (“used by 3 of 11”) and offers to clear the individual settings so your choice actually takes. Holiday hours are time pickers instead of typed text, one button sets the same hours on every holiday at once, and a new Minimum cover only option means a holiday gets exactly what you asked for and nobody else — not for hours owed, and not to fill the floor.

Two privacy faults found and fixed. The reason somebody was away — Sick, Parental leave, Leave of absence — was printed in the grid on the week PDF, the per-person print pack, the CSV and the workbook, so everyone learned everyone else’s. Every shared sheet now says only “off”; the reason stays on your own screens. And “send to everyone it applies to” by email put every person’s message in one body — whose leave was approved, whose was declined, by name — while the screen promised BCC kept them apart. BCC hides addresses, not words. It now opens one email per person.

Nothing goes out in half, and deletes clean up after themselves. A week emailed or texted to the whole team was silently cut at 1,800 characters (900 for a text) — a schedule that stopped somewhere in Friday — and the board then recorded the week as handed out. It now refuses rather than send half, and says what to do instead. Separately: deleting a role used to leave posts, shifts, ratio rules, patterns and holidays still pointing at it, so a post restricted to that role became unfillable by anybody, permanently, and the day sheet gave the reason as “not Any role”. Deleting a shift left posts pinned to it. Both now clean up every table. And moving your week boundary no longer throws away day-sheet notes, approved overtime, staff claims or the week’s pattern.

Holidays: say what actually runs. Reported by you. A holiday was drafted exactly like that weekday — a Thursday holiday got your ordinary Thursday, whole roster and all. Holiday cover did exist, but it lived on the Holidays page behind a dropdown and only counted once you had switched it on, so nobody found it. It is now the eighth day on Coverage needs: under Sunday to Saturday there is a Holidays block, edited by the same dialog as every other day, with the same “exactly this many” meaning. One press turns it on. And the week’s checks now name it either way — a holiday drafted like an ordinary weekday says so, and one carrying more people than you asked for explains that the floor still has to be covered and points at holiday hours.

A new shift is eight hours plus the meal. Asked for by you. Type 8:00 and the shift ends at 16:30 — eight paid hours plus the unpaid meal the law where you are owes for them. Move the start and the end follows it; type your own end and it stops following, so a deliberate twelve-hour shift is never quietly pulled back.

Fewer places to look. Asked for by you. Six “Set up” menu rows, four of which nobody could tell apart by name, are now one Schedule setup with tabs — shifts and roles, coverage needs, who gets first pick, posts on the floor. Time away joins Requests as a tab, so time off has one front door instead of five. And Business & law and Scheduling rules were quietly editing the same stored values as Settings through a second set of controls with different wording; they are now a statement of what is in force, and Settings owns the editing, so the two can never disagree. Every old link, fix button and search result still lands exactly where it did.

Busy stretches, and a menu that makes sense. Both asked for by you. You can now say when a day is busy, not just that it is: Coverage needs → a day → “Set a busy stretch”. Like the busy-day label it never adds anybody — it decides where the people you already have go, so hours owed land on a shift that covers the rush, and the checks tell you when the rush is the thinnest part of the day. Alongside it, a full pass over the menus: one Settings card had grown to 54 rows under the title “Meals and rest breaks” while holding pay periods, email setup and licences — it is now seven cards that say what they hold, the groups are named for one thing instead of two, a dead screen is gone, and on the phone Settings and the day sheet are at the top of the menu instead of the bottom.

Search actually searches. Asked for by you. Settings holds 89 rows, and the group you were on hid 81 of them — so searching for “pay period” found nothing unless you had already guessed which group it lived in, which is the one thing a search box exists to save you. Searching now looks across every group, and a row can be found by the name of the card it sits in (“business name” now finds it). Results still appear from the first character, narrow as you type, and go straight there when you press one.

Exports, checked at real size. Asked for by you. Tested against 150 people and twelve drafted weeks — 6,672 shifts. Two real faults fixed: a long table lost its column headings on page two and could have a row sliced in half by the page break, on every printed schedule and day sheet; and a custom report could only leave as CSV. Reports now export as a proper Excel workbook — real number cells so hours add up, a frozen bold heading, your subtotals and total, and a second sheet recording what the report covered and when it was run.

Coverage needs now opens on what the week you are looking at is actually drafted from. Reported by you. If a week is on a pattern, the page used to open on the standard week regardless — so you could add, remove or change a role on a shift, press Generate, and watch the week come back exactly as before. The change was real; it was written on a set that week never reads. The page now follows the week, and if you deliberately edit a different set it says so at the top with one press to switch back. Alongside it: when every row on a shift is exact, that is now the whole shift — nobody is quietly added beside them to make up somebody else’s hours.
