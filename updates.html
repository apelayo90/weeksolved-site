What's new

Weeksolved ships improvements continuously — most tester reports turn into a fix the same day. Here's what's landed recently, in plain words. Items marked Asked for by a tester came from somebody using it and telling us.

September 3, 2026

The week boundary is no longer a blind spot. Days in a row and the turnaround between two shifts were only ever counted inside one week. Someone who worked Friday, Saturday and Sunday and then came back Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday was six days in a row and nothing said so; closing on Sunday night and opening on Monday morning was six hours off and nothing said so either — because those shifts sat in two different weeks. The checks now read the week before, and say so in plain words: “6 consecutive days, counting the 3 at the end of the week before”. The seventh-day premium in an agreement is counted the same way. The drafter already avoided creating these; what was missing was the warning when a hand edit, a swap, a claimed open shift or a per-diem call-in created one.

An old saved week can no longer stop the checks. A shift saved by a much earlier build could carry a breaks record with no owed-break count on it; the checks read that count and stopped dead. Those shifts now have their breaks worked out again when the board loads, and the checks step over anything they still cannot read.

Lighter on an old phone, once you have a year of schedules. Undo keeps a copy of the whole board for each step, and a board with a year of weeks on it is nearly a megabyte a copy — forty of those was thirty megabytes of memory sitting behind every screen. The stack is now capped by size as well as by steps: forty steps on a normal board, fewer on a very large one, never more than a few megabytes, and a save that changed nothing no longer uses a step at all. Undo behaves exactly as before.

A crash nobody should have hit. With the doors set to close for lunch and meals timed on the schedule, one line of the checks referred to something that was no longer in scope — and the whole check run stopped, taking Home, This week, All schedules and Overview blank with it. Found by a new test that turns every optional feature on and off in random combinations and draws every screen. Fixed, and that combination is now in the permanent test set.

A week going up no longer disappears. While a posted week sits in its grace window, All schedules showed it nowhere — not in Drafts, not in Posted, not in the counts — and Home’s six-week strip called it a draft. It now has its own Going up section at the top of All schedules, with the countdown and a Take it back button, an amber card on Home, and a place in the tally.

Problem reports say which build you were on. Every report was stamped with a version number frozen four days ago, which made it impossible to tell whether a fix had reached the person reporting. Reports now carry the real build.

This keeps happening. Asked for by a tester. When the same flag shows up in two or more of the weeks on your board, Home now lists it under This keeps happening — a dip every Tuesday, one person over their cap week after week, a post with nobody to hold it at lunch. Same roster, same rules, same flag next week. Fix for good opens only the changes that stop it: a rule lowered or narrowed, one more person on that weekday, the doors shut for lunch, a cap or contract updated on a profile. A temporary fix each week is still a perfectly good choice, and nothing is ever changed for you — the point is that the pattern is pointed out so the schedule gets better over time instead of being patched every Monday. Repeats are also marked keeps happening in the week’s checks, and the Fix sheet says so at the top. Hide one you have decided to live with; Settings → This board turns the whole thing off.

Type the date. Asked for by a tester. Every date field — a birthday, a license expiry, a start date, a request, the vacation and starter forms — now takes typing: 9/15/2026, 09152026, 9-15-26, just 9/15, Sep 15 2026 or a pasted 2026-09-15 are all understood, slashes appear as you type and never fight the backspace, and a date that does not exist (2/30) turns red instead of silently becoming something else. The calendar is still one press away for anyone who prefers it. No more spinning a wheel back forty years to find a birthday. Phone numbers tidy themselves into (555) 010-0100 when you leave the box.

Show, not an eye. The password box now has a plain Show / Hide button instead of the eye icon. It is a switch, not a hold-to-peek: press Show and it stays shown until you press Hide.

Nothing scrolls sideways on a phone any more. Every wide table in the app — roles, named shifts, holidays, holiday pay, the analytics tables, the request list — now turns into a stack of cards on a phone, each line labelled, with every button on screen. Coverage needs lays each shift out on one wrapping line with Remove always reachable. The only thing that still slides is the hour-by-hour chart on the Day sheet, which says so, and the break sheet under it has every time as text. The open/closed switch under When you’re open now reads the natural way: on means open.

The roster fits the screen. Asked for by a tester. Team no longer scrolls sideways. On a phone every person is a card; on a desk the table fits the window. The days someone can work are seven small tiles — green for a day they can work, red and struck through for a day they never will — with the hours on hover or press, and a one-line key above the list. Full time, part time, per diem and in-training now read as words in their own colours everywhere they appear, and a declined request is labelled as such rather than just faded.

Every red thing explains itself. Asked for by a tester. Any coloured chip that means something is wrong — must fix, warnings, min short, over cap, under, a negative PTO balance, callouts, a license expiring, a request not honored, a solo day nobody can cover — is now a button. Press it and a short note says what it means and what to do, with a button that takes you straight to the fix or the right screen.

Forms open as pages. Asked for by a tester. Viewing the vacation form (and the starter, holiday, open-shift and ask-the-team forms) showed raw code on some devices. Each one now opens as a proper page in a new tab from a See the form button, and downloads with the right file type. All five were opened and filled in on a phone and a desk to make sure.

Fix from the review card. The “your pass before it goes up” card on This week now carries a Fix button beside each problem group, opening the same fix list as everywhere else, and picking Pharmacy or Clinic in setup pre-ticks the roles that need a license (Pharmacist and Technician; Provider) — untick if that is not how it works where you are.

“Bucket” is gone. Asked for by a tester. Setup used to ask you to sort people into buckets. They were roles all along — Pharmacist, Technician, Front counter — so that is what setup calls them now, with a one-line explanation and example names in the boxes.

Press Fix. Asked for by a tester. Every flagged problem now carries a Fix button — on Home, in the checks panel under This week, on every coverage dip on the Day sheet, and on the “no cover free” and “meal late” chips on the break sheet. It opens one short list: what fixes it this week (stretch a shift with the overtime priced, move a meal to a time the floor holds, waive a meal where the law allows, bring someone in, hand a shift to someone who is clear) and what stops it coming back (let a meal count as on duty for that rule, close the doors for lunch on solo days, take a day out of a rule, schedule one more person that weekday, change your hours, update a profile). One option is marked Recommended. Nothing is invented — every option uses the shifts, people and rules you already have — and the checks run again the moment you press one. No more digging through tabs to find the setting that would have fixed it. The short segments on the Day sheet’s coverage bar open the same list, a request that could not be honored has a Fix button under Requests (hand the shift over, make the request a must, draft again, or decline it on the record), and where a dip could be closed by scheduling one more of a shift you already run, that is a single press onto that weekday’s coverage needs.

A waived meal is not a missing one. A meal waived where the law allows it (a short shift, or the second meal on a day under twelve hours) was still being flagged as missing on the break sheet and in the checks. Fixed.

The sign-in check points at the problem. Having trouble? Check this device, on the sign-in screen, now lays its answers out as a list and marks anything wrong in red — storage blocked, no connection, cookies off — so the one line that matters is the one you see first. Copy it still sends the plain text to support.

September 2, 2026

Pasting a roster is forgiving now. Setup takes names one per line, and a list pasted from Excel, an email or a phone contact list brings tabs, commas, bullets, numbering, addresses and phone numbers with it. Those are now let go and the names kept — “Ann Smith, Pharmacy Technician” becomes Ann Smith, a header row called Name is skipped, and the same person pasted twice is counted once.

A real Excel workbook. Asked for by a tester. Export & share (and the Home screen’s Open it in Excel) now gives you one .xlsx with four tabs: the wall-chart week grid, one row per shift for payroll, paid hours by person as numbers that add up, and the day sheet. Bold headers, frozen title rows, no file to convert. The comma-separated files are still there for anyone who wants them.

Keep two people apart. Asked for by a tester. A grievance, siblings who cannot share a till, a separation, a legal order — open either person under Team, choose the other, and say how far apart: never on the same day, never on overlapping shifts, or the same shift is fine but never the same post or one that works alongside it. The drafter treats it as a rule it may not break, Cover the gaps never offers a blocked person, and a hand edit that breaks it is flagged in the checks panel. It is private to you: nothing about it appears on a printout, a message, a calendar file or an export.

Sign in with Google. Asked for by a tester. One press on the sign-in screen, no password to remember. If your Weeksolved account already uses that email address it is the same account — your board is exactly where you left it. New testers still enter their access code, right after Google confirms who they are. Under Settings → Your account you can attach Google to an account that has a password, or add a password to one that only has Google. Not yet on an iPhone or iPad, where Google’s window cannot reliably hand the answer back to the page — email and password work there as before, and the Google route follows shortly.

Older boards open cleanly after every update. A board saved before a feature existed could come back missing the settings that feature needs, and the first screen that looked for them could go blank. Every saved board is now filled in against the current defaults on the way in, whichever build it was saved by. If a screen ever went blank on you after an update, this was why, and it is fixed.

Labor cost now counts the way a pay run does. Daily overtime and double time first, then the weekly line applied to the straight time left over, charged on the day it crosses. Six eight-hour days now cost what payroll will actually pay. States without daily overtime can say so: Settings → Overtime thresholds, 0 for none, and picking a state in setup fills in the right lines.

Pay periods land on the right day all year. The day the clocks go forward is an hour short, and the pay-period arithmetic treated that as a whole day: from March to November every boundary sat one day late. Fixed, for anyone with pay periods switched on.

Monthly time-off accruals keep their day. Someone who started on the 31st was accruing on the 28th of every month after February. Each anniversary now counts from the start date.

A full browser is not a silent one. If this device's storage fills up, Weeksolved says so once, and says where your changes are still safe, instead of quietly dropping them.

American spelling throughout. License, color, honored, "check the box" — a few British spellings had crept into the app and the site. Gone.

An afternoon of pressing every button. Six hundred buttons on every screen, on a phone and on a desktop, on a full board and on an empty one; every screen in Los Angeles time across both clock changes; every saved-board shape from earlier builds. Two of the fixes above came out of it. Nothing else broke.

Reminders, in the calendar you already carry. Asked for by a tester. A license running out, next week's schedule going up, a poll closing, a holiday nobody has decided about — Weeksolved can now write those into your own calendar, so they reach you when the app is shut. It writes the entries; your phone delivers them, on its own terms, inside your own quiet hours. Before you commit to anything it shows you the entire list with dates, so you can see exactly how many you would get. The weekly one goes in as a single repeating entry rather than fifty-two, and two reminders on one day arrive as one. Nothing is emailed, nothing is texted, and nothing about your team leaves your device to make it work.

Put Weeksolved on your home screen, without hunting for how. Asked for by a tester. It was always installable — nobody knew. Now a single line offers it, once, on the device you are holding, with the steps written for that device: Safari, Chrome, Android or desktop. On the browsers that can do it in one tap, there is a button. Wave it away and it never asks again, and it stays under Help & FAQ if you change your mind.

Your five message templates all do something now. The one for "a posted week changed" was editable but never actually used — the app wrote its own sentence and quietly ignored yours. It now sends what you wrote, one message per affected person, with the change filled in. Delete a variable and it tidies up after itself rather than sending you a sentence with a hole in it.

Somebody who must never work alone will not be left alone by a lunch break. Two people on the same shift used to eat at the same time; spreading their meals apart could strand one of them on the floor. The meal spacer now treats that rule like every other rule it already respects.

Is your email address confirmed? Your account page now says, plainly, and offers to send the confirmation again if not. A mistyped address used to stay wrong and silent until the day you needed a password reset — which is the worst possible moment to find out.

Security and correctness work you will never see. A stored cross-site-scripting hole in the coverage table, closed. Reply codes and round tokens now come from the browser's cryptographic generator instead of ordinary randomness. The app refuses to open inside another site's frame. Small buttons on phones are now the same comfortable size as every other control. And the drafter was re-checked against 400 randomly generated businesses and all nine states' break rules — 7,182 combinations — to prove it never invents a shift you did not define and never places a break the law would not allow.

September 1, 2026

Staff under 18 are now scheduled lawfully, automatically. Switch it on, put a date of birth on anyone still at school, and tell Weeksolved your local school year. From then on it holds every one of their shifts to the limits for their age — how many hours on a school day, how many in a school week, and how late they may finish — and it won't schedule them outside it even by hand. Federal law is the floor everywhere and a state can only be stricter, so whichever is tighter is the one applied. Work permits and the hazardous-job list stay yours; the hours are ours.

Offer an open shift to the team. When a shift can't be filled, Weeksolved now writes you a one-page file and the message to send with it. Your people open it on their phone, check off anything they'd take, and it hands them a short code to send back. Paste the code in and you see exactly what they're offering — with every shift re-checked against hours, turnaround, availability and licenses before you confirm it. Still no accounts for staff, still nothing for them to install, and ticking commits nobody until you say so.

You can see every device on your account. Settings now lists each browser your login has been opened in, when it was last used and roughly where — with a way to remove one you don't recognize, or sign every other device out at once. If the account is in use in several places at the same time, you get a quiet note about it. Optional on top of that: an emailed confirmation the first time your account is opened on a device it has never been used on. If that email can't be sent for any reason, the check turns itself off and lets you straight in — it can never lock you out of your own schedule.

Every change to a posted schedule is now on the record. Once a week goes up, anything you change afterwards is written down — what moved, when, and whether the people affected were told. It sits on the Record page, exports to CSV, and can't be edited by hand. Keep it 90 days, a year, two years, or forever; you decide in Settings.

A note that belongs to one shift. Truck at 7am, a hand-over, training with a new hire — type it under someone's name on the day sheet and it follows just that person, just that day: on the printed sheet, in the PDF, in what you send out, and inside their calendar file.

Their phone reminds them, not you. Choose how far ahead — a couple of hours, twelve, or the day before — and the alarm rides along inside the calendar file each person gets. It goes off on their own phone. Nothing for them to install, no account for them to keep, and nothing for you to remember to send.

August 31, 2026

Cover a dip with one tap. When coverage dips during a day, the day sheet now offers to stretch a shift already on the schedule — keep someone later or bring someone in earlier — with any overtime priced before you decide, least disruptive option first. It never invents a shift and never breaks your longest-shift rule.

Holidays got their own brain. Setup now asks whether business rushes the day before a holiday, after, or both — rush days draft busier automatically. And the holiday itself can now run like the weekday it falls on, carry its own coverage pattern you define once, or sit closed entirely.

Weekends rotate fairly. Tell setup whether weekends are days people want or days they want off, and the sought-after-shift rotation spreads them accordingly — no more the same person stuck with every Sunday.

Guaranteed minimum hours. Every person now carries a minimum next to their cap, and the drafter fills everyone to their guarantee automatically — flagging anyone it couldn't. Team setup asks for both, with sensible defaults by employment type.

Smarter defaults for your kind of business. Pick pharmacy, restaurant, clinic, warehouse, salon, hotel, security, office and more — and setup pre-answers break handling, holiday behavior, weekend rotation and shift preferences the way that industry usually runs. Everything stays visible and changeable; only the starting answers differ.

Add it to your home screen. Weeksolved now installs like an app: on iPhone, Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android, Chrome → menu → Add to home screen. Real icon, full-screen, same account everywhere.

Updates come to you. When new settings arrive, existing accounts get a one-time, one-minute prompt with just the new questions — your setup, schedules and team always carry over untouched.

Phone polish and tightened security. Buttons no longer collide with the browser's own controls at the bottom of the screen, sideways scrolling is gone across every screen size, staffing hours are now clearly asked as the window people work (prep before open, close-down after), and account access records were locked down harder behind the scenes.

August 30, 2026

Accounts and sync. Sign in once and your board follows you between phone, tablet and computer — with sync rebuilt to stay fast even after months of schedule history.

Help that answers back. A built-in assistant answers questions in seconds, the ⚑ Report button files a problem in ten, and Contact us reaches a real person without opening your email app.

The compliance core. State meal and rest break rules applied while drafting, staggered meals that never drop coverage below minimums, license expiry tracking, printable day sheets that show where each break was placed, PTO ledgers, fair rotation you can see at a glance, and a scheduler that only ever uses shifts you've defined.

Spot something that should be on this list — or something broken that shouldn't be anywhere? The ⚑ Report button in the app goes straight to the fix list. Several of the entries above started as one line from a tester, which is the honest reason this page keeps growing.
