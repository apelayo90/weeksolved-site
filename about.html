Built on the counter it schedules

Weeksolved started in a working California outpatient pharmacy, where the week's schedule was a pile of requests, a grid of constraints, and an evening lost to fitting one into the other.

Every scheduling tool we tried had the same shape: a pretty empty grid, and good luck. The rules that actually make scheduling hard — licensed coverage that can never lapse, meal breaks that have to land inside a legal window, ratios between roles, the person who can't close Tuesdays, the fairness argument about who gets stuck with Sunday — all of that stayed in the manager's head. The software just held the boxes.

So we built the other tool: one you teach your rules once, and from then on the week drafts itself — breaks placed lawfully, coverage honored, requests worked in, and a plain-language explanation for anything that couldn't be. It never invents a shift you didn't define, and when something won't fit, it tells you why and shows you the ways out with the least disruptive one first.

What we believe

Staff shouldn't need accounts. Only the manager signs in. Everyone else gets their schedule the way they already live — text, print, email, or straight into their phone's calendar. Nothing to install, nobody to onboard, no per-seat bill creeping up with every hire.

Compliance is how the schedule gets built, not a report you run. Break law differs by state and the penalties are real. Weeksolved applies your state's rules while drafting, so problems get caught before the schedule goes up — not after a claim lands.

Pricing should be flat and boring. One price per location. Hiring your 30th employee costs the same as your 8th. No seats to count, no surprises to audit.

Your data is yours. Private to your account, exportable any time, never sold, never mined for ads. Leaving is allowed to be easy — that's what makes staying a choice.
